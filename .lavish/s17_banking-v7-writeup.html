Write-up · v7 played on all 60 train and 37 test tasks · one trial each

v7 passes 37 of 60 train tasks but 12 of 37 test: its workflows help the questions they were written from

v7 is v6, which looks up workflow_rag's library before it answers, forked to Sonnet at medium effort. Against the champion v4 (Sonnet, high), it passed 37 of 60 train tasks against 29, and 12 of 37 test tasks against 14. It halved the cost per conversation, but the library it reads cost about as much to build as that saved on one pass.

08 · Caveats — four things this comparison cannot separate

  1. Effort and customer changed with the workflows. v7 ran at medium effort against a Sonnet customer; v4's runs used high effort and a Haiku customer. On smoke, v4 scored 11 of 20 with either customer, so the customer matters less than the rest.
  2. One trial each. The test difference, 2 fixed against 4 broken, has p = 0.69; train's 12 against 4 has p = 0.077. Neither clears 0.05.
  3. Train is not held out for v7. workflow_rag read each train question's customer script, so 26 of 60 train conversations followed a workflow written for them; test is the only honest measure here.
  4. Two cost lines are estimates. The hidden lookup calls and the library build are priced from rates, not billed amounts; both together are $91.84 of v7's $174.40.

09 · Closing — keep the tooling, do not promote v7

What the branch keeps

  • workflow_rag r1 and its live demo on the Agent tab, with gold read only by the score.
  • v6's two harness tools, find_workflow and request_workflow, and live conversations that stream them.
  • A fork that keeps workflows:, and a lookup that weighs rare words over "credit card".
  • Evals listing all 97 questions with their split, test's workflows mapped by rule.

What it does not show

  • That a cached workflow helps a question it was not written from: test fell from 14 to 12 of 37.
  • That the fallback works: request_workflow never ran in a scored conversation.
  • That workflows fix card transaction disputes: v7 passed 2 of the 18 tasks that need one, across both splits, against v4's 1.

If the idea is reopened

  1. Make a lookup say no: a match needs most of a job's name, else request_workflow.
  2. Build the train library leaving each question's own session out, so train measures what test does.
  3. Play v4 at medium effort with the Sonnet customer, so only the workflows differ.

v4 stays champion. v7 is a held experiment: none of its runs used the gate's customer, so the gate never read them.
