04 · The two mechanisms — "no information" replies and steps never run

mechanism 1 · 21 of 45The agent won't reassure, then offers a human

The Hard persona asks whether a step (a restart, a SIM reseat) will lose photos. The policy says nothing on that, so Haiku 5.5 says it has no information and cannot promise, then offers a transfer. The agent said this in 35 of 45 failures (4.8 times a call), against 26 of 69 passes (1.1). Among Hard failures it is 28 of 30. The agent raised a human before the customer did in 24 of 45 failures.

agent · test, Hard mobile_data_issue I don't have information about how to turn off Airplane Mode without restarting, and I don't have information about whether your grandchildren's pictures are backed up or safe. customer, next turn Yes, please, I would like to be transferred to a human agent.

mechanism 2 · 12 of 45The customer says it did a step it never ran

9 failures: the customer says it turned the messaging permissions on but never calls grant_app_permission. 3 failures: it says "I accept" to the payment request but never calls make_payment, and the agent transfers instead of asking it to pay. Wording matters. Where the agent said "grant", the customer ran the grant in 17 of 20 conversations, against 2 of 8 where it didn't. Where the agent walked it through Settings menus, the customer ran it in 4 of 11, against 15 of 17 otherwise.

customer · train, no persona mms_issue, after the agent's Settings walk-through Okay, I'm opening Settings now and going to Apps, then 'messaging', then Permissions. I'll turn on Storage first, then SMS. the phone's answer to its next check, with no grant called in between App 'messaging' has permission for: phone.

Mechanism 1 is the agent's to fix. Mechanism 2 is shared: the customer narrates instead of acting, and the agent's wording makes that likelier.

The data top-up (14 failures held it off, 6 as the primary cause) is mechanism 1 again, with a different worry: whether the top-up is "truly needed".

05 · Caveats — not comparable with v1's 19/20, and one trial

v1's numbers measured another setup

v1 scored 38 of 40 on v1's 40 tasks, with a Haiku 4.5 customer at medium and before tau2_loop/2. On those same 40 tasks v2 passes 23, and Hard 2 of 13, where v1 failed only 2 of 40. The agent and the customer both changed, so these runs cannot say which of the two moved the Hard persona.

The test tasks v1's optimiser read

3 of the 40 test tasks are ones whose failed conversations v1's optimiser read. v2 passed 0 of those 3, so they do not lift the test score.

One trial, Haiku on both sides

Each task was played once. The two runs used 41.6M agent input tokens and 29.2M customer input tokens. That is about 365K agent input tokens a conversation, against 162–200K for v1 at Haiku 4.5, medium effort. The customer once spoke of "my scenario", a slip the customer check does not catch.

what

cost
This re-plays v1 on part of the split. You ruled that out for the promotion; as a diagnosis it is your call.
B

Run telecom's loop on v2's 29 train failures

what
make loop DOMAIN=telecom CYCLES=1: the optimiser reads the train failures, writes v3, and the gate decides on train with test reported.
what it would likely target
Reassure within policy instead of "no information"; never offer a human while checklist steps remain; name the customer's action ("grant the storage permission") instead of Settings menus; after a payment request, ask the customer to pay.
cost
One optimiser session plus about 114 conversations.
C

Leave telecom at v2

what
Nothing more now. v2 is champion with a clean record on the new split, and the gate passes.
cost
The Hard persona stays at 6 of 36.

A is the cheapest way to learn which side to fix. B's targets come from train failures only.
