Plan · workflow_rag r2 on banking's 60 train questions · nothing built, nothing run

r2 should merge each question into the workflow it shares, then check it

r1 wrote 118 workflows from 60 train questions and the library keeps 64: a later session's workflow replaces an earlier one whole, so 17 of the 60 questions have none of theirs left. r2 runs the questions one at a time. Each session researches its question, merges what it found into the current version of every job it shares, writing to a rubric its prompt carries, and passes deterministic checks before the library takes the new version; a reviewer comes later, with optimising. Can a workflow's quality be scored? Yes, modestly: on the 59 v7 train conversations that read a workflow, the share of the documents' tools those workflows name predicts pass or fail (AUC 0.69).

02 · The build — one question at a time, merged then checked

Fig 2 · r2's build loop · each box is a stage of one question's turn
match

Same job, or new

The harness offers the library's candidates for each drafted job: the same name, an alias, or the lookup's top three by name. The agent says “merge into X” or “new job”, with a reason, so four retention names become one job with branches.

merge

Current version and draft, side by side

The agent gets vK in full, its own draft and the harness's diff of the two (steps, tools, rules in one and not the other). It writes vK+1: every step of vK kept unless a quote removes it, the new question's case added as a branch with its condition, and a changelog of what it added, changed or removed.

gate

Checks, sent back once, then taken or not

D8 and D1 run without a model: nothing lost that the changelog does not name, no quote that is not found. A failure goes back to the merge once; a merge that still fails is not taken and the library keeps the previous version. The rubric is in the writer's prompt, so it writes to it; the reviewer waits for optimising.

Sessions stay immutable folders under rag_agent_runs/, each holding its merged versions; since each version contains the one before, the library's latest-wins rule becomes correct as it stands. One at a time: r1's sessions took a median 3.8 minutes and ran side by side, so research, merge and review in turn for 60 questions is about 8 to 12 hours (an estimate).rag_agent_runs/*/run.json duration_ms

05 · Can quality be scored? — yes, modestly; tool coverage predicts pass




A · no modelThe deterministic rubric
D1–D8 on every version, in seconds. On r1 against v7's train outcomes: tools referenced AUC 0.69 (95% CI 0.55–0.83, permutation p 0.004); the mean of the six computed checks 0.66 (0.52–0.80, p 0.020).


B · the reviewerThe judged rubric
J1–J5, each pass or fail with evidence. Before its marks count for anything, measure them the same way on train: an item with no link to outcomes and no policy reason is dropped.


C · outcomesThe answering agent on the questions
The pass rate of conversations that read a workflow: the truest signal and the noisiest. It mixes job difficulty, the customer and the question's own workflow, so it checks A and B; it is not the score. Test, once, is the final measure.




What does not work: the share of gold's writes a question's workflows cover scores AUC 0.46; failing conversations' workflows covered more (0.96 against 0.88), as s19 found: failures make every gold call and one more. Caveats: 59 conversations, so wide intervals, and job type is a confounder: the dispute jobs fail most and name the most tools. Research agrees on the shape: graph matching against gold workflows (WorFBench's WorFEval), outcome-grounded metrics (SOP-Bench), and binary checklists for judges (CheckEval).scratchpad s20/quality.py · 5,000 permutations, 2,000 bootstrap draws


Score with A after every merge, keep B only where it agrees with C on train, and leave the verdict to test.

08 · Risks — bloat, order, gold, train inflation, time




Bloat
Twelve savings questions merge into one workflow; it can grow past what the answering agent reads well. J2 and J4 ask for branches, not repeats, and a size budget per workflow is a check the reviewer must meet.


Order
A bad early version becomes the next merge's start. D8 and the changelog make every change visible, the reviewer runs after each merge (Q3), and the order is recorded, so a second order can measure the effect.


Gold reaching workflow_rag
A check that sent back “question 055's gold tool is missing” would teach gold to the writer. D8 uses the previous version; gold coverage is only shown. Q4 decides how much of v7's outcomes the reviewer sees.


Train inflation
v7 and a v8 read workflows built from the same train questions they are scored on, and a reviewer that reads outcomes fits them more. Train overstates; test, held out, is the measure.


Time
One at a time is slower than r1's side-by-side batches: about 8 to 12 hours for 60, from r1's median 3.8 minutes a session plus a merge and a review. Questions sharing no job could run together later.


Grading its own work
The reviewer fixes and marks the same workflow. Its marks are evidence, not the gate: D1–D8 run again on what it wrote, and it cannot edit the checks.
