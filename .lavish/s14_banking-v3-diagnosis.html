the short version

The diagnosis. Opus 5.5 (medium, 28 turns) calls 36 of 48 failures knowledge, 4 lost state, 3 format, 2 write arguments and 3 the task's fault. It routes 39 to system.md, wants v2's prompt and helper back, and expects to fix 19.

Against the leaders. They pass 18 of v3's 48 failures reliably (6–8 of 8), 14 sometimes and 16 never. I agree with 30 of the 48 diagnoses, disagree with 3 (all three “task faults” are tasks a leader passes), and would defer 15 whose rules fit tasks no leader passes.

Retrieval. The tools are not the problem: the leaders found the tool names v3 missed with the same BM25, by searching each step of a procedure. v3 searches half as much (10.7 look-ups a conversation to 24.0–24.9), acts after 3.1 of them to their 9.8–13.7, and has read 49% of the required documents by its first write to their 69%–78%.

What I would change. Ten proposals in §7, none applied. First P0: a probe that plays each train task only to the agent's first reply, scored against the leaders' first turn (they make 7.9–12.7 look-ups before replying, v3 2.6). Its levers are the leaders' own: let a turn keep all its tool calls, then raise reasoning effort and model toward theirs. Both leaders ran tau2's stock agent with v3's exact prompt. Beyond the leaders (each marked as a deviation, your choice per row): guardrails in hooks for transfer counting, procedure order, the verification time and one credit per account (Fig 12), v2's helper, value discipline, and three fixes to what the optimiser itself sees.

Agents research in a loop over a research state, and the leaders keep that state in their context window

Every recursive-research design runs the same loop. What differs is where the state of the research lives: in the transcript, in a ledger beside it, or in a separate agent.

  1. Plan. Turn the request into the questions that must be answered (here: the procedure's steps, and the tool for each).
  2. Query. Search for the first open question.
  3. Read. Open the best hits in full, not just the snippet.
  4. Extract. Pull out facts (rules, amounts, argument names) and leads: names the text introduces that are not yet understood (a tool, another document, a step, an eligibility rule).
  5. Update the state. Record what is now answered, with its source, and add the new leads.
  6. Stop or follow. Stop when every question has a source and no new lead appears, or a budget runs out. Otherwise take the next lead and go to 2.
