The PIN-lock key gets 7 facts and 4 traps from one train task, and K3, the suspicious period, is the one 090 keeps failing on

This is the answer key the merge bench would grade the second job against, as the debit key does for the first. It checks whether a version of pin_lock_investigation_protocol leads the answering agent to what task 090's gold does, and whether it states a wrong rule found in an earlier version. Task 090 is the only train task about a locked PIN, and r18's 090 session is the only train merge into the job. So the key rests on one customer with three locked cards, and the bench would replay one merge. v23, v26 and v27 all lost 090 the same way: they disputed the customer's own Boston transactions from before the overnight attempts. K3 is that cell. r18's 090 draft had the right rule, r18's merge dropped it, and r19's turned it into trap T4. Nothing runs until you approve the key in §06.

What it tests wellwhether a merge carries the 090 draft's fixes (K3, K5, K6) without its traps (T2, and T3 before the closure)—
  • What it would cost. Two trials each of B and D-lean is four merges: about 0.55M Opus output at s32's rates (D-lean about 90K a merge, B about 185K), plus three grader calls to check the hand labels and one a version.
  • What must be built first. A bench.yaml entry for the job (seed r17, r18's 090 session, order [task_090]). Per-job baselines in merge_eval.BASELINES, which now point r17 v0 at the debit job's session; this job's r17 v0 is in the r3 task_092 session. The key moved into merge_bench/banking_knowledge/eval/keys/.
  • The alternative I looked at. closing_cancelling_a_debit_card had six r18 merges, four of them train (the same 084, 088, 090, 086), and r18 took only one. Its gold spans eight train tasks. But in the six of them v27 played, it closed each card with the right reason in all but 082. So it is a failing merge more than a failing job.
