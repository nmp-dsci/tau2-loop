04 · Why the library let them through — the rubric checks that a quote exists, never that it supports the step

The 8 misreads: every quote is real, and none supports its clause

The rubric's D1 looks each quote up in the document it cites (workflows/rubric.py). Nothing compares a step's do or if with that quote. A step carries one optional quote for its whole do, which may hold any number of conditions, values and prohibitions. A merge keeps an old step on its own word: D12 passes it when the merge lists it as kept. J1–J5, the judgment items, are graded by the model that wrote the workflow, and there is no reviewer. So all 8 failing steps scored 1.0 and nothing was sent back.

No cheap check separates a misread from a sound step, because the defects are conditions and scope, not figures. Your rule makes it checkable: every clause references where it comes from. Then a clause no document gives has nowhere to hide (M1). A reviewer reads the clauses the checks flag (M2), and a kept step meets the same checks as a new one (M3). M1's checks catch all 6 defects. They also flag half of today's steps, which is the size of the library's debt, not noise to tune away.
