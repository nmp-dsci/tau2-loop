Banking's leaders run the stock agent: they read before they act

The top of τ-banking's board is not custom engineering. 27 of its 28 text entries are Sierra running tau2's unmodified agent, with the strongest model at its highest effort and AllTools retrieval: BM25, dense search and a read-only shell over the knowledge base. Qwen 3.8 Max leads at 55.2% pass^1 and Claude Opus 5 follows at 48.7%, on 97 tasks × 4 trials. Your v2 scores 22.7% on one trial of the same 97 tasks, and v1 8.2%. Their 776 published conversations show what the stock agent does with those tools that yours does not.

the short version

Before their first action, the leaders make 9 to 13 look-ups to your 2 to 3. They open 10 to 14 documents by name through the shell. They have more of the needed documents in hand. With every needed document read, they still pass 62–66% against your 38–40%. So the gap is half retrieval and half acting on what was read. Your harness adds two defects they don't have. A third of v2's replies are tool calls written as text, which never run. In 20 of 97 conversations it calls a discoverable tool by its bare name, which the grader never sees. §6 lists twelve changes, harness first.

why yours has no shell and no INDEX.md

Neither was ruled out on its merits. s09's plan dropped AllTools because its dense search calls OpenAI embeddings, which the subscription can't call. Its retrieval question (Q1) then offered only bm25_grep or bm25, so the read-only shell went too, though it needs no key: tau2 runs it locally through Anthropic's sandbox-runtime (srt) and ripgrep. Neither is installed on this machine yet. INDEX.md isn't one of the 698 documents. tau2's sandbox writes it when it exports the documents as files for the shell, so an agent without a shell has no file to read. Your grep tool regex-searches the same documents and returns up to 10 whole documents ranked by match count, never a list of titles. tau2 also has no stock variant with BM25 and the shell but no embeddings: alltools requires dense search and terminal_use is the shell alone. That's why K1 registers one from your runner..lavish/s09_banking-loop-and-surfaces-plan.html Q1 · vendor/tau2-bench/src/tau2/knowledge/sandbox_manager.py: export_documents, _create_index_file · src/tau2/domains/banking_knowledge/retrieval.py: RETRIEVAL_VARIANTS · retrieval_mixins.py: grep

dense search · it needn't be OpenAI's

AllTools uses OpenAI's text-embedding-3-large because that's tau2's default. tau2 ships two embedding backends, OpenAI and OpenRouter (Qwen3-Embedding 4B or 8B, Gemini's embedding model), and both are paid web services that need a key. Anthropic has no embeddings API, so the subscription can't make embeddings by any route. A model running on your machine needs neither, so s09's step from "the subscription can't call OpenAI" to "no dense search" didn't follow.

The closest open-source choice is Qwen3-Embedding (Apache 2.0), the family tau2's own alltools-qwen variant calls through OpenRouter. The 0.6B size runs comfortably on this M1 Pro with 32 GB; try 4B if search quality falls short. tau2 embeds the 698 documents once and caches them, then each search embeds one query locally, with no key and no bill. It does add a model that isn't Claude to the setup, which the billing rule ("every model call runs on the subscription") doesn't foresee: that's your call.

Wiring it in leaves tau2 untouched. Our runner adds an embedder class that follows tau2's BaseEmbedder (embed, get_name), puts it in the two lookup tables tau2 builds embedding pipelines from (both can be extended from outside), and registers a variant from tau2's own all_tools_variant pointed at it. That is AllTools exactly: BM25, dense and the read-only shell. It needs sentence-transformers (which brings PyTorch) or the lighter fastembed, plus srt and ripgrep for the shell.

Expect the least from it of the three. In the paper, dense alone averaged 16.88–17.11 against BM25's 17.04 and the terminal's 19.20. In the leaders' conversations the shell does most of the work: Opus 5 makes 17.9 shell calls a conversation to 3.4 BM25 and 2.8 dense, and Qwen 3.8 Max 13.2 to 4.9 and 4.7. Add the shell first. Dense can join the same v1 re-score, which is cheap since you're re-scoring anyway.vendor/tau2-bench/src/tau2/knowledge/embedders/ (openai_embedder.py, openrouter_embedder.py, base.py) · document_preprocessors/embedding_indexer.py and input_preprocessors/embedding_encoder.py: EMBEDDER_REGISTRY · src/tau2/domains/banking_knowledge/retrieval.py: all_tools_variant · arxiv.org/html/2603.04370v1 Table 2 · board transcripts, scratchpad summary.json

your harness · a third of v2's replies are failed tool calls

Your agent sends tool calls as JSON in its reply text. In v2, 517 of 1,690 agent replies (31%) were tool calls written as plain text instead, in 79 of 97 conversations. A slipped call never runs. v2's helper swaps each one for "One moment please…", the customer answers it, and a turn is gone. That's why v2 carries 18.0 customer messages against the leaders' 7. v1 slipped in 147 of 1,017 replies (14%, 55 of 97 conversations). Failed conversations slip more than passed ones (5.7 against 3.9 a conversation in v2). The leaders call tools natively, so they can't slip.

Five lessons, generic enough to give an optimiser

  1. Search the customer's stated problem first, before generic gates like verification and before picking a remedy (033, 019, 021).
  2. Search hits are a sample, not the catalogue. For "best", "compare" and "audit" requests, list the whole product family and read each member (025, 064, 073, 096, 100). BM25 + grep can't list by filename, and the shell can.
  3. Every discoverable tool goes through unlock, then call, even when its name is already known (044, 047, 048, 051).
  4. Do what was asked, then every step of the retrieved procedure, and re-check eligibility per item before each write (079, 089).
  5. Ask neutral questions for argument values, and confirm an ambiguous answer before writing (031).

Eight of the fifteen need a document BM25 never ranked. Each time, the leader found it by listing a product's files and reading them.
